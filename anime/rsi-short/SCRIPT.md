# 「黙れ、ロジャー」 / *Damare, Roger* — 30s anime short

Adapted verbatim from a public X thread (@alessandrod × @TheWatt...). Every line of
dialogue is a direct Japanese rendering of what each person actually posted — the
comedy comes from staging a petty subtweet as an end-of-the-world shonen climax.

- **Runtime:** 30.0s · **Format:** 1080×1920 (9:16) · **Audio:** Japanese VO · **Subs:** burned-in English
- **Shots:** 6 · **Tone:** Evangelion-grade melodrama collapsing into deadpan

## Why this reads as fluid and not as AI slop

The failure mode of generated anime is asking for big full-body action and getting
melting limbs. Real TV anime is mostly **held drawings with moving effects**. So:

1. **Keyframe first, animate second.** Every shot is a still image locked for
   character consistency, then driven by image-to-video. Never text-to-video —
   that is where faces change between shots.
2. **One motion per shot, and make it a camera move.** Push-in, dolly, whip-pan.
   Camera motion reads as expensive; character motion reads as broken.
3. **Move the cheap things:** hair, coat hem, steam, petals, cloth, clouds, screen
   flicker. Leave faces and hands alone.
4. **Cut on the impact, not through it.** Hard cuts on beats 3 and 6 hide any
   interpolation artifacts exactly where the gag lands.
5. **Shot 5 is deliberately frozen.** A true hold after four moving shots is a
   comedy beat *and* a free shot.
6. **24fps, then hold frames on impacts** — matches limited-animation cadence
   instead of the floaty 30fps smear that makes AI video look cheap.
